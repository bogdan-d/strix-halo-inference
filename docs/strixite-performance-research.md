# Strixite performance and correctness research

Research date: October 11, 2026. Canonical upstream is `shawnshekari/strixite`; source inspection used the read-only clone at `/home/bogdan/Frameworks/strixite`, verified HEAD `d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136` (October 10). This report concerns Ryzen AI Max+ 395, gfx1151, 128 GiB CachyOS, existing 105.46875 GiB GTT, preferably 262,144-token capacity. No model, engine, build, upstream script or benchmark was run, and no host setting or live Pi configuration was changed. Container, model-file and Pi integration implementation are outside this report.

## Decision

**Worth a bounded third-engine experiment, not a replacement or a demonstrated speed upgrade.** The publisher targets exactly this chip/model and reports unusually strong deep-context decode and substantial prompt-cache work. However, it is a roughly week-old public project, its full claimed test/benchmark suite is not shipped, forced tool selection is unsupported, and an independent user already reported severe memory-pressure failures. Use v0.2.3 or a later explicitly revalidated revision, one engine at a time, and 256K capacity initially; do not copy the publisher's host kernel/BIOS settings or assume its 512K defaults fit our environment. Sources: [platform and headline claims](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/README.md), [memory guidance](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/memory.md), [server limitations](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/server.md), [v0.2.3 release](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/releases/tag/v0.2.3).

Registry inspection verified v0.2.3 digest `sha256:9075d3def69d550b6c8f0b42a7795259ec02187ebf755027149f8c757d6cc738`, build timestamp `2026-10-10T18:43:45Z` and OCI revision `dbe7b66`. ## Performance: what is actually supported

- **Publisher measurements, not our tests:** README reports approximately 47–50 tokens/s decode from zero to 492K context, approximately 1,370 tokens/s prefill over a real 490K conversation, and 19/19 terminal-bench-mini core tasks. The explanatory page reports 48.9 tokens/s at 0–128K versus 48.5 at 384–512K with drafting, and plain decode slowing 2% from 64K to 224K. These are session aggregates and selected tasks, not matched comparisons with our engines; task definitions, complete trajectories, independent scoring and reproducible public benchmark artifacts were not established. Sources: [README](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/README.md), [deep-context explanation](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/how-it-got-fast.md#4-why-the-speed-stays-flat-at-492k).
- **Better controlled, still publisher-only:** [v0.2.0 release](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/releases/tag/v0.2.0) compares tagged v0.1.0 and v0.2.0 builds on the same machine/afternoon, two runs per depth, reported median: prefill 1,547→1,602 tokens/s at 32K and 1,494→1,555 at 120K; decode 54.8→57.6 and 53.7→57.0 respectively. The author explicitly warns decode includes thinking and MTP and changes with generated content. This demonstrates the author's within-engine measurement practice, not superiority to Gufo or Halogen.
- **Architectural reason for flatter decode:** 36/48 layers carry fixed-size Gated DeltaNet state; 12 attention layers use an indexer selecting 512 blocks of four earlier tokens (approximately 2K keys). Indexer scoring still grows with depth. The n-gram table is read from SSD with RAM row caching and prefetch during drafting, making drive performance and cache warmth consequential. Source: [architecture and I/O explanation](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/how-it-got-fast.md#4-why-the-speed-stays-flat-at-492k).

## Correctness and public test coverage

- **Additional Pi configuration blocker:** request parsing rejects nonzero `presence_penalty`/`frequency_penalty` and `repetition_penalty` other than 1. Our Pi OFF profile's `presence_penalty: 1.5`, identified by the integrating agent, cannot be copied unchanged. Ordinary Pi auto tool use is plausible but unverified; API compatibility is not end-to-end provider validation. Source: [parse_common penalty validation](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/serve/openai.cpp#L144-L146).

- **Quantization evidence is narrow:** the publisher reports top-token agreement 226/242 positions and mean KL 0.028 against full precision across three short/long/chat prompts; this is a quantization study using the original forward with rounded weights, not an exhaustive validation of the shipped GPU engine or coding quality. Experts and GDN input use four bits; most dense projections use eight bits; activations and KV use BF16. No calibration/imatrix is used. Different quantizers and weight layouts mean no equivalence with our GGUF or Halogen packages can be inferred. Source: [weights and study methodology](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/weights.md#how-much-accuracy-it-costs).
- **Reproducibility gap:** README says every kernel is checked against a CPU reference; CONTRIBUTING says the author runs a full test suite and before/after benchmarks. In this checkout there is no `tests/` directory and no public benchmark/study source tree: CMake conditionally enables development tools only when their sources exist and adds tests only if `tests/CMakeLists.txt` exists. The public `tools/` directory contains converters, inspector, server entry point and `check_stream_calls.cpp`, not the named model benchmark programs. Thus claims of comprehensive testing cannot be independently reproduced from this pin alone. Sources: [contribution policy](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/CONTRIBUTING.md), [CMake conditional development/test targets](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/CMakeLists.txt#L160-L167), [conditional test registration](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/CMakeLists.txt#L339-L342), [public tool directory](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/tree/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/tools).
- **Positive public parser evidence:** [merged PR #5](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/pull/5) documents buffered/streamed parsing equivalence, randomized/tokenizer/byte fragmentation cases, zero failures over 20K-case runs on 14+ seeds, nine mutation tests, and scripted-backend OpenAI-client checks. The checker source is public at [tools/check_stream_calls.cpp](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/tools/check_stream_calls.cpp). These are author/contributor results, not tests performed here, and parser equivalence is not model/tool-use quality.
- **Blocking feature for current validation parity:** `tool_choice: required` and named-tool selection return HTTP 400; enforcement is only roadmap work. JSON response constraints cannot be combined with tools and reject many schema keywords, including numeric bounds, `pattern`, `format`, `allOf`, conditionals and `uniqueItems`. Vision is not implemented; concurrent conversation execution is not shipped. Sources: [server contract](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/server.md#structured-output), [roadmap](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/roadmap.md). Our existing forced-tool checks therefore cannot pass unchanged; ordinary auto tool use must be checked separately rather than silently claiming parity.
- **Tool safety caveat:** malformed or truncated streamed calls may retain unclosed arguments, with `finish_reason` and `strix.malformed_tool_calls`/`dropped_partial_tool_call` indicators. Clients that repair partial JSON can execute truncated commands. Strict final argument validation and refusal of length-cut calls are prerequisites for agent use; Pi-specific handling is not verified by this research. Source: [streamed-call contract](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/server.md#streamed-tool-calls).
- **Behavior differs from raw model decoding:** thinking nudge is enabled by default and inserts text when repetitive thinking crosses thresholds; thinking budgets can forcibly close the think block. Disable nudging for like-for-like comparisons and record thinking settings. The parser maps `minimal` to `low` and `high` to `xhigh`, supports `medium`, and uses `none` for off. The chat template inserts the selected effort into the system prompt. This is not a distinct numerical thinking budget for each level. Sources: [thinking controls](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/server.md#thinking), [request parser](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/serve/openai.cpp#L43-L49), [template effort](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/serve/chat_template.cpp#L105-L109).

## Long context and fit on this host

- **Native versus extended:** the model's trained capacity is 262,144; 524,288 uses YaRN factor 2. The author claims no measurable quality cost from YaRN, but the inspected public evidence does not establish broad retrieval accuracy, multi-needle performance or coding reliability at near capacity. Flat generation speed is not proof that distant information is used correctly. Source: [YaRN explanation](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/how-it-got-fast.md#4-why-the-speed-stays-flat-at-492k).
- **Publisher memory accounting:** default 512K capacity and 16K prefill chunk allocate 95.3 GiB GPU buffers at startup, plus approximately 3 GiB host n-gram row cache; GPU buffers do not shrink just because the current prompt is short. Halving capacity to 256K reportedly frees approximately 6.7 GiB, suggesting approximately 88.6 GiB GPU buffers by subtraction, not a measured allocation here. Against 105.47 GiB GTT that leaves nominal buffer headroom, but pinned export buffers, other GPU users, physical RAM, driver accounting and peak pressure prevent a guaranteed fit claim. Source: [memory accounting](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/memory.md#where-the-128-gb-goes). Upstream's tested host uses approximately 124 GiB GTT, Fedora 43 and TheRock HIP 7.16 rather than our host environment: [README](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/README.md#quick-start).
- **256K is upstream's desktop recipe:** `capacity = 262144` and `prompt-cache-ram-margin-gib = 8`; a full saved state is approximately 6.8 GiB and the startup check requires margin plus one full state, approximately 14.8 GiB MemAvailable after loading. Values are from the author's machine. The fail-by-default check happens after weight/GPU allocation, so it is not proof that allocation itself is safe under an undersized GTT budget. Keep existing host settings and assess actual startup accounting first. Source: [desktop recipe and startup check](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/memory.md).
- **Caching matters for real agent latency:** saved conversation state costs approximately 27 KiB/token plus 0.12 GB; author reports approximately 1-second RAM resume and a few seconds from disk. Defaults cap disk cache at 128 GiB, retain 32 GiB disk free and budget writes at 32 GiB/hour; eviction can drop a state and cause full re-prefill. This can dominate conversation switching even when decode stays flat. Clean stop may flush for up to 40 seconds; ordinary 10-second Podman stop can interrupt it. Persistent state compatibility excludes changes to weights, activation type, MTP on/off and YaRN factor. These are upstream behavior/measurements, untested locally: [cache, budget and shutdown guidance](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/blob/d6060e3b4a0a659066b01cf227f897624ead6136/docs/memory.md).

- **Independent real-use report, not a general success benchmark:** [issue #6](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/issues/6) by `enncoded` reports v0.2.0 on Void Linux, gfx1151/128 GB, TheRock 10.1.0rc3, Pi with 59 tools and a co-resident approximately 23 GiB 27B sidecar. Its healthy request log shows 63,599 prompt tokens, 618 generated tokens at 51.8 tokens/s and a tool call, followed by SVM mapping failures/segfault at approximately 0.4 GiB MemAvailable and earlier aperture violations near 1.5 GiB. Issue #6 is closed by the maintainer; no reporter rerun establishing resolution on the affected environment was present in the fetched comments. Do not claim that crash class is independently fixed. Source: [release fixes](https://github.com/shawnshekari/strixite/releases/tag/v0.2.3).

## Comparison with existing local evidence and acceptance gates

[Our local validation](validation.md), especially lines 42–62 and 134, establishes Halogen 0.17.5 and Gufo 0.11.0 startup at 256K, real conversation/tool/cancellation checks, Pi end-to-end read/edit/bash loops, and a 40,030-token synthetic prompt. It explicitly does not establish near-full 256K retrieval or matched-engine throughput. Gufo's reported approximately 88.5 GiB startup GPU allocation and Strixite's inferred approximately 88.6 GiB are different accounting/environments and not a measured equality. Likewise, the local 23.2/25.1-second padding requests cannot be ranked against Strixite's real-conversation prefill claims.

Before deciding to keep a third engine, require actual fit at 256K without host tuning, strict streamed-tool parsing and cancellation/post-cancellation checks, a Pi tool-loop test, MTP versus plain greedy consistency on uncached prompts, long realistic coding/retrieval prompts at increasing depth, restart-cache compatibility/integrity checks, and isolated memory peaks including cache export and shutdown. For performance, alternate engines over identical tokenized inputs where possible, track sampling/nudge/thinking/MTP and cache state, separate prefill/decode/TTFT/end-to-end task latency, and score outputs with independent checks. Near-capacity quality and peak memory remain explicit unknowns for all three engines; Strixite's unsupported forced tool choice remains a specific parity blocker, not something performance numbers erase.
